The Claim

The thesis as of July 13, 2026, and where I differ from the consensus

The crowd: has basically stopped moving. Polymarket's 2026-signing market sat at ~46-47% on July 11 and is still at 46¢ on July 13 (44 bid / 46 ask) — but on just $5.9K of 24-hour volume, thin enough that "unchanged" may just mean nobody's trading this week. Kalshi's longer-dated ladder is mixed: the far end softened (July '27 51¢→46¢, January '28 56¢→51¢) while the near end firmed a touch (January '27 35-36¢→37¢, April '27 41¢→43¢) — low-conviction noise, not a repricing. The loud story hasn't changed either: still no cloture motion, and the merged Banking-Agriculture text has now been called "imminent" in reporting dated July 9, 10, and 12 without actually dropping. Then, the night of July 11, Sen. Lindsey Graham died suddenly — an event nobody priced in and, so far, nobody has re-priced around either.

Me: draft ~50% for the next month — back to a true coin flip, not a bullish tilt — and still ~92% that a crypto rules law exists within 3 years.

My edge: unchanged since June — the crowd keeps pricing a timing problem like a death problem. The House did its half a year ago (294-134). A merged text is still coming, just later than three straight rounds of reporting promised, and the fight is still down to one variable — an ethics deal — and one-variable problems get solved or slipped, not killed. What's genuinely new is a wildcard I can't cleanly quantify: Graham's death, combined with Sen. McConnell's continued absence, shrinks the GOP's effective floor margin to as low as 51 for the time being, on top of a calendar that was already tight. No reporting connects that to CLARITY specifically — this is my own inference, not a fact on the page — but it's the real reason the 1-month score came back down rather than held.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • The market has stopped believing "delayed, not dead": Kalshi's 2027–28 ladder still prices passage by April 2027 at just 43¢ and by January 2028 at 51¢ — down a notch from a week ago on the far end. If the smart money thought this was a pure timing problem, those long-dated contracts would be far richer.
  • Three strikes on "imminent": the merged text was called days-away in reporting dated July 9, July 10, and July 12 — and it still hasn't shown up as of July 13. If sources this close to the process keep guessing wrong on timing, my own confidence in "week of July 13" should come down, not sit flat.
  • The calendar just got harder, not just brutal: ~19 usable days, cloture already filed on the NDAA (not CLARITY), judicial nominations stacked first, still no floor date — and now the Senate is short-handed at the worst possible moment. Sen. Graham's sudden death and Sen. McConnell's continued absence put the GOP's effective floor count as low as 51 for now. No one has reported that this delays CLARITY specifically, but a thinner margin on an already-packed calendar is not a reason for optimism.
  • The dealmaker isn't at the table: the White House hasn't signed off on the merged text, and CoinDesk's July 12 column reports it's been less engaged lately than earlier in the summer. A rewrite tilting toward consumer protections could as easily lose Republican votes as gain Democratic ones — and Trump is already playing signature-hostage on another bill.
  • The ethics fight got harder, not easier: the only compromise on the table dropped out of negotiations entirely, the $1.4B presidential crypto disclosure is a gift to every Democrat who wants to say no, and Gillibrand's "no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" meets a White House that refuses any Trump-specific language — immovable object, irresistible force, an already-thin calendar.
  • The midterms are still the real wall: miss August 7 and September belongs to election politics. Worst case — Congress resets in January and the Senate starts its bill over.
