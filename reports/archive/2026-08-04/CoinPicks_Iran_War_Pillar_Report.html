The Facts

As of July 30, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page. This is a full weekly research refresh covering the Jul 25-30 gap, independently re-verified against primary/multi-source reporting rather than carried forward from either contradictory daily touch.
  • The lull was real, then it broke: Iran's IRGC struck a US/CENTCOM base at Muwaffaq Salti, Jordan (Jul 28). Confirmed by 5+ independent outlets (Al Jazeera, CNN, Axios, RT, JPost) — ballistic missiles/drones, described as a "surprise attack," all intercepted by US/Jordanian defenses, zero US casualties. This came after a genuine 2-3 day pause in CENTCOM strikes (Jul 25-27) and on the same day Trump was publicly touting "very friendly" talks with Netanyahu at the White House — both things happened.
  • The US retaliated with a "heavy wave of strikes" (Jul 29) — explosions reported at Bandar Abbas, Kish Island, Qeshm Island, and Khuzestan Province (CNN). Trump: "it's our turn to hit them." CNN's Jul 30 live coverage explicitly frames this as "US renews strikes on Iran as widening war pulls in more countries."
  • NEW FRONT — Iraq: US and Saudi Arabia conducted joint strikes on Iran-backed Popular Mobilisation Forces across Iraq (Jul 29), CENTCOM-confirmed, hitting sites in Baghdad, Wasit, Nineveh, Basra, Kirkuk, Karbala, and Diyala — 20 killed, 32 wounded (Al Jazeera, CBS News, Foreign Policy, 5+ outlets). Iraq's government called it a "flagrant violation of sovereignty." This is Saudi Arabia's first turn as a direct co-belligerent in this war. A claim that "six Iranian IRGC advisors" were also killed is single-source (unnamed Iraqi militia officials) — flagged, not used.
  • NEW FRONT — Egypt: a drone hit a US-owned FSRU vessel and a tanker at Egypt's Damietta port (Jul 29) — the first strike on Egyptian soil in this war (Al Jazeera, CNBC, Times of Israel). Fire contained, no injuries. No group has claimed responsibility; Houthis explicitly deny involvement — attribution remains unresolved.
  • Brent rebounded from the lull-low. Brent fell to ~$90/bbl (-6.7%) during the Jul 25-27 lull, then rose back to ~$92-93/bbl by Jul 30 (+~3.5% day-over-day, +24.6% month-over-month) on the renewed strikes and widening war (Fortune). A previously-circulated "$87.53" figure could not be corroborated and is dropped from this report.
  • Iran continues rejecting a direct-talks framing even as Trump claims progress. Iran FM spokesman Baghaei, Jul 27: "We have no negotiations with the United States at present" — directly contradicting Trump's public claims of productive talks. A regional 10-day ceasefire proposal (Qatar/Egypt/Oman/Pakistan) remains on the table, unaccepted; Iran separately rejected an Oman joint-Hormuz-management proposal on Jul 28, insisting on controlling its own lane.
  • Congress split on competing War Powers resolutions (Jul 23, carried forward, no new action since): House passed a nonbinding one 214-208 (4 GOP defectors); the Senate's binding one failed 49-47.
  • The Pentagon casualty-count dispute (18 vs. 14) is confirmed stale, not a fresh Jul 30 event. The DCAS toll briefly rose to 18 (Jul 23) then reverted to 14 (Jul 24) amid a credibly-sourced dispute (NYT officials, Rep. This is the same dispute already logged last cycle — one daily touch this week re-cited the Jul 24 CNBC article as if it were new; it is not.
  • Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to normal commercial traffic — ~10 transits/day versus a normal baseline near 88/day (IMF PortWatch), unchanged from prior cycles. IRGC, Jul 30: the Strait "will remain closed."
  • No formal MoU withdrawal by either side. The 60-day window to ~Aug 16 remains intact and un-triggered.
  • Israel remains out of direct engagement — no confirmed direct Israel–Iran exchange since June 7–8; the widened fronts (Iraq, Egypt) involve the US, Saudi Arabia, and unclaimed actors, not Israel.

This week's live test is the strongest evidence yet of desensitization — and the sternest. That is a remarkably muted reaction given the news, even accounting for Brent's rebound to ~$92-93. A desensitized market means shallower dips and smaller Vs, and this week is the starkest example yet, even as the underlying war has gotten wider and, arguably, more dangerous in absolute terms (Saudi Arabia is now a direct combatant). Still a thin sample; the Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the lean is worth acting on.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • This report's own daily checks contradicted each other this week — that's a real quality problem, not just noise. One touch said the war was cooling with "good chance of progress"; the other said Iran had launched a surprise attack. Both were true, but a system that can produce flatly contradictory reads two days apart deserves real skepticism about what it might be missing right now.
  • The war just widened to two new countries — that is not desensitization, that is escalation. Saudi Arabia directly striking targets in Iraq for the first time, and an unclaimed drone hit on Egyptian soil, are structurally different from "another strike night" — new state actors and new territories are exactly the kind of tail risk that can turn a contained conflict into a regional one.
  • Brent's rebound to ~$92-93 after a lull is itself a warning sign, not just a data point — it shows the market is already pricing renewed risk after barely two days of calm. Goldman's scenario work put Brent above $100 in 2026 if Hormuz stays largely shut for a month; a third straight week of Hormuz effective closure (~10 transits/day vs. ~88 baseline) is consistent with exactly that kind of sustained disruption.
  • Egypt's strike attribution is unresolved — if it's confirmed as Iran-linked, that's a much bigger deal than this report is currently scoring. A strike on Egyptian soil, if attributed to Iran or an Iranian proxy, would represent the war's first extension to North Africa and a US-aligned state outside the immediate Gulf theater.
  • Thin sample, still. The bullish long-term read rests on ~2 dip-and-recover events, both in a bull regime — geopolitical shocks don't always V-recover, and a war that just added two fronts in 48 hours is not obviously past its worst point.
