The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • The market has now confirmed a real repricing, not thin-volume noise: three independent outlets logging the same record-low ~30-32% read is a much stronger signal than the single thin-volume Polymarket print this report was leaning on two cycles ago. If the smart money is genuinely this bearish on 2026 passage, the structural "delayed, not dead" framing deserves more skepticism than this report gives it.
  • The correction on Graham's seat cuts against the bull case too, in a subtle way: if the GOP's floor math was never as thin as this report assumed, then the ethics deadlock alone — with no seat-count excuse — has been enough to stall the bill for weeks. That's arguably a worse signal about how intractable the core dispute is, not a better one.
  • The NDAA-queue explanation doesn't have an end date either: "the week of Jul 20 at the earliest, Jul 27 as a fallback" is still just a target, and this report's own history is three-plus rounds of missed "imminent" and "targeted" dates in a row.
  • The dealmaker isn't at the table: the White House hasn't signed off on the merged text. A rewrite tilting toward consumer protections could as easily lose Republican votes as gain Democratic ones.
  • The ethics fight hasn't moved an inch: the $1.4B presidential crypto disclosure remains a gift to every Democrat who wants to say no, and Gillibrand's "no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" still meets a White House that refuses any Trump-specific language.
  • The midterms are still the real wall: miss August 7 and September belongs to election politics. Worst case — Congress resets in January and the Senate starts its bill over.
