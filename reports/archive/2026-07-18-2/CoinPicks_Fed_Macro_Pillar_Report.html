The Claim

The thesis, and where I differ from the consensus — updated July 18, 2026, full weekly refresh

The crowd: caught between two stories that don't fully reconcile. The June CPI (-0.4% m/m, largest drop since Apr 2020) and PPI (-0.3% m/m) prints, plus Fed Chair Warsh's measured Jul 14/15 testimony, pushed Jul 28-29 FOMC hike odds down to ~12-17% (from ~46-47%) — a clean, multi-sourced dovish signal. But the September question is a mess: this cycle's independent re-check found three different market-implied reads that don't agree — a same-day Investing.com Fed Rate Monitor snapshot (~57.2% cumulative hike odds, more hawkish), a Slatestone-relayed figure (~17%, far more dovish), and a third source (~41.5%). Nobody has reconciled these.

Me: near-term improves to a genuine Mild Bear rather than outright Bear (34→42 at 1mo) on the July-meeting signal, which is real and well-corroborated. I'm holding 3/6/12/36mo rather than moving them further, because the September figure that would justify a bigger move is contested three ways and I don't have a way to adjudicate it independently this cycle — per the sufficiency rule, a genuinely unresolved market-implied number shouldn't drive a re-rate until it's pinned down.

My edge: the crowd is fixated on the September question; I think the July meeting itself was the more decisive near-term signal and it just resolved dovish. The plumbing story from prior cycles (TGA spend-down, reserve build, QT effectively dead) remains directionally intact — I did not find a fresh H.4.1 release with a primary-confirmed figure this cycle to update it further, so it's carried forward rather than re-verified from scratch. That's why the 6-month and 1-year windows still sit above the 1-month, and why the 3-year P holds near 61-62.

The Prize

Illustrative only — not price targets, not advice

The canonical anchor: 2020-21 ZIRP + QE ran BTC ~$3,800 → $69,044 (~15–18×) — a unique COVID/zero-rate/open-ended-QE setup, not a repeatable base case. The 2024-25 cuts ran ~$60K → a ~$126K ATH (Oct 6, 2025) — but that top came on a non-Fed tariff shock and has since roughly halved to the low-$60Ks ($63,992.32 on the July 18 close). From here, a retest of the $126K high ≈ +95%.

Discount it hard, twice. First: easing is not an automatic catalyst — the last cycle topped on a non-Fed shock. Second: the pivot that unlocks any of this is now less clearly pushed out than a week ago — the Jul 28-29 FOMC hike odds collapsed to ~12-17%, meaning a hold (not a hike) is now the heavily favored outcome — but the September question remains genuinely unresolved. The Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the 3-year lean is worth acting on when the tide finally turns.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case my bearish lean is wrong
  • I may be over-crediting one dovish signal. The July-meeting hike-odds collapse (~12-17%) is real and multi-sourced, but it's one data point; if the September figure resolves toward the more hawkish of the three conflicting reads found this cycle (~57.2% cumulative), the "improving" story could reverse quickly.
  • The labor market may still force the Fed's hand in the other direction. The June payrolls miss (+57K, −74K of revisions) argues for cuts, not hikes — but a hike into a cracking labor market, if it does happen in September, would be a policy error that raises long-run conviction even as it hurts near-term.
  • Two of three unverified data points this cycle could each move the score if confirmed wrong. If the daily draft's H.4.1 Jul 16 figure ($3.143T) turns out inaccurate, or if the core PPI figure (4.7% y/y) is materially different from what BLS actually published, this cycle's read would need revisiting.
  • Crypto has now shrugged off a full week of Iran-war escalation, not just a few days. BTC held flat ($63,796→$63,992) through the Iran pillar's worst week of the conflict — either genuine decoupling, or complacency ahead of a bigger shock.
  • Latent liquidity positives remain queued but unverified this cycle: the TGA spend-down and Warsh's balance-sheet task force (still single-source) could loosen policy without a rate cut — not independently re-checked this cycle.
