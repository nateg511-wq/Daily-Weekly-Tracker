The Claim

The thesis, and where I differ from the consensus — updated July 10, 2026

The crowd: "the ceasefire is over" — Trump repeated it Friday, in capitals. ~170 targets struck inside Iran in two nights, bridges hit for the first time since April, IRGC missiles at four countries including Jordan — its first strike there since June 11, Hormuz on the US Navy-led JMIC's highest "severe" advisory. The loud story is pure risk-off: stay out until it's over.

Me: near-term I'm still with the bears — 42% good-for-crypto at 1 month, because a strike-pause-strike regime with a severe-shut strait can re-fire any night. But the tape beat the worst case: zero US casualties, retaliation that was almost fully intercepted (performative, not lethal), a Friday lull, and Iran asking to continue talks. The arc holds: 83% over three years. The dip is still a buy — it just isn't fully formed yet.

My edge: the market is telling you what it thinks — oil fell to ~$76 despite 170 targets struck (de-escalation being priced), and BTC decoupled and rallied +3.5% to near $64K on July 10, explicitly on chips, yen, and dollar weakness, not Iran. The risk isn't the headlines the crowd fears; it's the quiet paths — a US-casualty event, a formal MoU death by ~Aug 16, a confirmed full Hormuz closure. I watch those, not the noise.

The Facts

As of July 10, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page. ★ = new since July 8
  • Second strike wave, July 9: after ~80 targets on July 8 (Sirik, Qeshm Island, Bandar Abbas area), CENTCOM struck ~90 more targets early July 9 — second consecutive night, ~170 cumulative — across Tehran, Bandar Abbas, Bushehr province, Chabahar port, and Iranshahr airport, plus, for the first time since April, bridges (railway bridges on the Tehran–Mashhad route / Golestan province). Stated rationale: "degrade ability to threaten freedom of navigation." Iranian Health Ministry: 14 killed, 78 wounded over the two days (incl. 3 killed at a Sirik fishing pier).
  • Iranian retaliation, July 9 — wider geography, near-zero effect: IRGC/army drones and missiles at four countries: Kuwait (Arifjan / Ali Al Salem — a Patriot battery targeted; 3 ballistic + 1 cruise missile + 10 drones all intercepted; 1 person injured, stable), Bahrain (Juffair / Sheikh Isa fuel depots — intercepted), Qatar (early-warning/satellite sites — no impacts reported), and Jordan — first time since the June 11 Azraq strikes: 10 ballistic missiles at Al Azraq Air Base, 8 intercepted, zero casualties or damage.
  • Zero US casualties either day — the single most important non-escalation fact of the week (the one Kuwait injury was not specified as US personnel).
  • Friday July 10 — a contested lull: no US-confirmed strike wave overnight Thursday–Friday, but Iranian state media reported fresh US strikes near Asaluyeh (South Pars energy hub) — unconfirmed by the US, with outlets explicitly flagging conflicting reports on any pause. Some US-official-attributed reporting describes a deliberate strike-then-pause posture to let diplomacy work. Trump, Friday: Iran "asked us to continue 'talks.' We have agreed to do so… but the Cease Fire is OVER!"
  • Khamenei buried; talks may resume July 11: Khamenei was buried early Friday July 10 at the Imam Reza shrine, Mashhad (some July 9 wires said July 9 — dating conflict noted; July 10 is the later/primary report). Talks are reported to resume July 11 — Al Arabiya via a Times of Israel liveblog: single-source relay, not confirmed by Qatar or State. Qatari negotiators traveled to Tehran in coordination with the US, and a mediator swarm has been active since Wednesday (Qatar PM–Araghchi call, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UN Guterres).
  • No formal MoU withdrawal by either side (explicit as of July 10). Iran calls the US strikes a "gross war crime" and an MoU violation and says it won't negotiate under attack — yet per Trump it requested continued talks. The 60-day window to ~Aug 16 is intact. (Signing date: June 17 "Islamabad MoU" per the better-sourced reports; one outlet says June 14 — conflict noted.)
  • Hormuz — worst readings of the crisis: the US Navy-led JMIC raised its transit advisory from "substantial" to "severe" — the highest tier, "deliberate hostile action" likely. Thursday traffic "virtually ground to a halt" (Insurance Journal); volume estimates conflict — ~50% of pre-war (Al Jazeera) vs a ~92% drop (Sunday Guardian, weaker source) — with transponders off obscuring the truth. War-risk cover now 2–6% of hull value (vs ~3% on July 8; wartime peak 10%), quotes priced hours before transit; syndicates advising a freeze on bookings west of the strait. The US publicly insists Iran "does not control" the strait. Background: Iran declared the strait shut ~June 22; pre-attack throughput was ~39–41% of pre-crisis per the unofficial straits.live tracker — single-source.
  • Oil FELL despite 170 targets struck — de-escalation being priced: Brent ~$78.02 July 8 → ~$76.3–77.0 July 9 (settle $76.30 per CNBC; TradingEconomics $76.97) → ~$76.00–76.80 July 10 (−$2.45 d/d per Fortune's 6:15am ET print — that delta and the ~$77 July 9 level reference different snapshots); WTI under $72. Attribution: the pause plus talks hopes. Brent never took out the ~$80 June 19 baseline.
  • BTC decoupled and rallied: July 9 it held above $62K with a "muted reaction" while oil rose and gold fell; July 10 it rose +3.5% to near $64K (intraday low ~$61,850), +4.2% on the week (7-day per CoinDesk; Fri–Fri trackers show ~+2.8%), ~$28B volume; ETH $1,760 (+2.6%). Attribution per CoinDesk: Asian chip/AI rally, yen +0.6%, weak dollar, liquidation-driven reversal — explicitly not Iran-driven. (That is a market move plus reporter attribution, not an institutional estimate.)
  • Oil re-sanctioned — wind-down on track: Treasury revoked the MoU oil waiver July 7 (GL X → GL X1): no new Iranian oil sales; in-process deals get grace to July 17, proceeds into blocked interest-bearing accounts; China/India refiners re-exposed to secondary sanctions. No new developments July 9–10.
  • How this round started — tanker attacks, night of July 6–7: the IRGC fired ≥2 missiles at commercial ships in/near the Strait of Hormuz. Two merchant vessels hit that night: Qatari LNG carrier Al Rekayyat (struck ~8nm off Limah, Oman — engine-room fire, crew abandoned ship) and Saudi crude tanker Wedyan (damaged); a third vessel was hit within 24 hours — three hit in total — and four more tankers turned back. Tehran did not officially claim responsibility.
  • The pattern: on June 27 an Iranian one-way attack drone hit the tanker M/T Kiku (Panama-flagged, 2M+ bbl of crude); the US struck targets in Iran the same day, and the IRGC hit US sites in Kuwait and Bahrain on June 28. The ceasefire has now cracked at least three times since April (June 7–8, June 27–28, July 6–9) — the rounds getting closer together and bigger.
  • Diplomacy background: indirect technical talks in Doha, June 30–July 2 ended with "positive progress" claimed but no breakthrough; two concrete outcomes — agreement to release the first $3B tranche of ~$6B in Qatar-held frozen Iranian funds, and an MoU-violations reporting channel. Sticking points: Hormuz transit fees (Iran: will "definitely" charge; US rejects) and the rest of the frozen funds.
  • Israel is out of this round: no direct Israel–Iran exchange since June 7–8, and Israel was not a participant in the July 7–9 strikes (Netanyahu visited Haifa naval base July 7, "eye to eye" with Trump). A July 10 Times of Israel liveblog report of Israel joining the strikes is single-source and unconfirmed — treat as rumor. Israel–Hezbollah simmers on (Rubio announced an Israel–Lebanon "framework deal" June 26) but is not the July driver.
  • The odds: Polymarket "US invades Iran before 2027" last verified at 14.5% (up from 11.5% on the strikes) — a ~July 8 print; no fresh July 10 print obtained, label stale. "Hormuz traffic normal by July" collapsed to 4.5%; "regime falls in 2026" ~2.45% (both ~July 8–9, secondhand). "MoU withdrawal by Aug 15" (26% on July 8) and "final deal by Dec 31" (35.5% on July 8): current prints unverified — Polymarket pages unfetchable July 10.
  • The baseline (still true, still load-bearing): June 2025 "12-Day War" — BTC dipped to a ~$98,225 intraday low (June 22, 2025), reclaimed $107k+ by June 25, a fast V. The far larger 2026 regime-change war (Feb 28 → Apr 8, 2026; Khamenei killed Feb 28, confirmed by Iran March 1) still didn't crater crypto on Iran-specific grounds.

The Prize

Illustrative only — not price targets, not advice

The upside is still the "buy-the-invasion" rebound. Anchors: Russia–Ukraine (Feb 24, 2022) — BTC fell ~8% to a ~$34,300 intraday low, posted a +14.5% up-day on Feb 28, ended ~+25–29% above the low within a week (~+38% by late March). June 2025 rhymed: ~$98k dip, fully recovered within a week.

The live test just returned its first reading: July 8's escalation bought only a ~2–3% dip to ~$61.7k, and by July 10 BTC was back near $64K, +4.2% on the week (7-day per CoinDesk; Fri–Fri trackers show ~+2.8%) — though that rally is attributed to chips, the yen, and liquidations, not an Iran V, so don't score it as one. A desensitized market means shallower dips and smaller Vs. The bigger prize is the settlement scenario: a signed deal plus the reported ~$300B reconstruction fund re-pricing the whole region after oil re-sanctioning. Still a thin sample; the Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the lean is worth acting on.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • BTC is a risk asset here, not a safe haven — and the oil-shock path still has numbers. Goldman's April scenario: Brent averages >$100 in 2026 if Hormuz stays largely shut another month ($120 Q3 / $115 Q4); JPMorgan: $120–130 if disruption persists through Q3, ~$150 in a full-closure worst case. Brent at ~$76 today doesn't retire those scenarios — the strait is on a "severe" advisory with traffic at crisis lows; if that persists, the inflation shock arrives late, not never, and crypto falls with equities.
  • It keeps breaking. The ceasefire has now cracked at least three times since April — June 7–8, June 27–28, and the July 6–9 round that grew to ~170 targets and put bridges on the target list for the first time since April. The rounds are getting closer together and bigger. Each one, "time heals" gets directly contradicted.
  • The rally may be complacency, not resilience. BTC rising through 170 strikes proves nothing about Iran — the move is attributed to chips and the yen. The market is pricing a contained flare-up (Brent $76, below the June baseline); that pricing is fragile to a US-casualty event, a formal MoU withdrawal, or a confirmed full closure — any one repricing gaps down, not drifts.
  • Thin sample, still. The bullish long-term read rests on ~2 dip-and-recover events, both in a bull regime — geopolitical shocks don't always V-recover.
