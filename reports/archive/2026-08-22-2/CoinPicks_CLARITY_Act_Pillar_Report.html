The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • The Tillis-Gallego "breakthrough" isn't confirmed by either side. No text is public, the White House hasn't signed off, and CoinDesk's own reporting hedges on whether the senators have actually reached an agreement. This report is giving real weight (an 8-point 6mo improvement from the Jul 18 low) to something that could evaporate as easily as the Jul 20-22 ethics deal did when seven Democrats rejected it.
  • A second unresolved fight just opened up. The developer/software-liability dispute, rejected by both the White House and industry Jul 30, shows that even as one axis of the ethics fight narrows, new disputes keep appearing — a pattern, not a one-off.
  • A cloture filing with no Democratic votes lined up may just be political theater. Punchbowl's own sourcing says Democrats consider this vote "doomed" — if Thune is filing cloture knowing it will fail, the point may be to put Democrats on the record before the midterms rather than to actually advance the bill, which would make today's "progress" more symbolic than substantive.
  • The floor-calendar problem doesn't have an end date either. "No path before recess" was confirmed by Thune himself, but this report's own history is repeated rounds of missed "imminent" and "targeted" dates — nothing guarantees the actual Sept 15-16 vote produces real movement rather than another procedural loss, and September is also when midterm-campaign politics starts eating floor time.
  • Prediction markets are genuinely more bearish than a month ago. A record-low 27% print, even if it partially recovered, is real information — Galaxy Research's own house view dropped from 50% to 30% independent of any single news event, suggesting professional handicappers see this as a genuine coin-toss-or-worse on 2026 passage.
  • The SEC's "we'll do it ourselves" signal cuts against urgency. If the White House and industry believe the SEC can deliver workable rules unilaterally, that reduces the political cost of letting CLARITY slip further — a slow bleed on legislative priority, not a dramatic kill.
  • The midterms are still the real wall: miss August 7 and September belongs to election politics. Worst case — Congress resets in January and the Senate starts its bill over.
