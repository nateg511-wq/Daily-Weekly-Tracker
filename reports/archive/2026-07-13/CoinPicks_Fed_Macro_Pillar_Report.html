The Claim

The thesis, and where I differ from the consensus — updated July 10, 2026; re-checked July 11, 12, and 13 (unchanged for a fourth consecutive check)

The crowd: "Inflation and the Fed are back in play" (Yardeni, July 8) — and the June minutes (released July 8) confirmed it: the Committee dropped its easing-bias language and "a few" participants wanted to hike at the June meeting itself. Futures still price a September hike at 50.8% for +25bp (~66% including +50bp, CME FedWatch, last read Jul 9) and a 78.5% hold for July 29 (Jul 13 read) — but Polymarket's own September ladder disagrees sharply, pricing a hike at just 38.5% (No change 56.5%, cuts a 3.75%+2.1% tail) — a 25+ point gap between the two market-implied measures that nobody has resolved. Now layer on the loud story: hawkish Warsh, a reignited Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz war (Brent back toward $79 as of July 12–13), and a widely-circulated — but wrong — claim that core inflation just hit a three-year high of 4.2% (it's actually 2.9% per BLS; the 4.2% figure is headline, not core). The crowd's conclusion: crypto stays dead indefinitely.

Me: near-term the crowd is right — my draft P is 34–36 out to 3 months, held flat for a fourth straight check. Liquidity's tide is still out. My edge is the sequencing: the July 2 jobs report shows the labor market visibly cracking (+57K with −74K of revisions and participation at a 2021 low), while the July 9 claims print (215K — no layoff wave, still the freshest read) says the crack is slow-motion, not sudden. Warsh has publicly staked his chairmanship on price stability, so the Fed will hike into that weakness before it eases — the worst near-term order of events for crypto, but a policy-error hike raises the amplitude of the easing cycle that follows. The Iran/Hormuz re-escalation is a real, corroborated upside-inflation risk into the incoming CPI/PPI/testimony/H.4.1 gauntlet — but per the sufficiency rule, an event that hasn't yet shown up in actual Fed or inflation data doesn't get to pre-empt the data itself, so it isn't moving this pillar's P today.

My edge: the crowd is pricing the hike; almost nobody is pricing what comes after it — and the plumbing flipped from drain to inject in early July and has held that way since. The TGA has been spent down to $749.2B (−$131.0B w/w, H.4.1 Wednesday level, re-verified directly against federalreserve.gov this cycle), bank reserves jumped +$132B in one week to $3.099T, the domestic ON RRP is effectively empty at $2.75B, QT is dead — and Warsh's balance-sheet task force (single source) still opens a path to ending runoff earlier. That's why the 6-month and 1-year drafts sit above the 1-month even as all five windows hold flat this check, and why the 3-year P holds at 61.

The Prize

Illustrative only — not price targets, not advice

The canonical anchor: 2020-21 ZIRP + QE ran BTC ~$3,800 → $69,044 (~15–18×) — a unique COVID/zero-rate/open-ended-QE setup, not a repeatable base case. The 2024-25 cuts ran ~$60K → a ~$126K ATH (Oct 6, 2025) — but that top came on a non-Fed tariff shock and has since roughly halved to the low-$60Ks ($63,796.08 on the July 12 close, dipping intraday toward the low-$62Ks amid the Hormuz shock). From here, a retest of the $126K high ≈ +98%.

Discount it hard, twice. First: easing is not an automatic catalyst — the last cycle topped on a non-Fed shock, and a Dec 10, 2025 cut drew a sell-the-fact dip. Second: the pivot that unlocks any of this remains pushed out — the Fed is priced to hike before it eases. The Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the 3-year lean is worth acting on when the tide finally turns.
