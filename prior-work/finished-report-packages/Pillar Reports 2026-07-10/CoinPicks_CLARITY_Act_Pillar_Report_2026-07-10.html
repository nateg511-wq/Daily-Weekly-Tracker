The Claim

The thesis as of July 10, 2026, and where I differ from the consensus

The crowd: repricing down hard. Polymarket's 2026-signing market slid from 46–47% (July 7–8) to ~40–41% by July 10 — with −4.5¢ of that on July 10 alone (live API, 15:58 UTC). Kalshi's by-end-of-2026 contract fell from ~46¢ to ~34–36¢ (thin contract, moves intraday), and its brand-new 2027–28 ladder (launched July 8, thin) prices even eventual passage barely above a coin flip. The loud story: no cloture motion, the NDAA owns week one, and the market has stopped treating passage as inevitable-but-delayed.

Me: draft ~50% for the next month — a genuine coin flip — but still ~92% that a crypto rules law exists within 3 years.

My edge: unchanged since June — the crowd keeps pricing a timing problem like a death problem. The House did its half a year ago (294-134). And this week produced the strongest evidence yet for my side: a merged Banking–Agriculture text with 70+ pages added is reportedly dropping the week of July 13 with floor action targeted for the week of July 20 (single reporting thread). That is what "negotiating over how, not whether" looks like. The fight is still down to one variable — an ethics deal — and one-variable problems get solved or slipped, not killed.

The Facts

As of July 10, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page
  • The calendar, exactly — now confirmed by a primary source: no cloture motion on CLARITY and no floor date as of July 10 — the bill has sat at Senate Calendar No. 423 since June 1. The Senate's own floor schedule: reconvene 3:00pm Monday, July 13; judicial nominations first (Schwartz post-cloture, a Jones confirmation vote ~5:30pm); Thune has already filed cloture on the motion to proceed to S.4784 (FY27 NDAA) — the NDAA owns week one, exactly as assumed. Last session day before the August break: Friday, August 7 — roughly 19–20 working days. TD Cowen (June 29): procedural steps the week of July 13 or July 20, with July 24 the critical date so the House can act before its own recess. One July 7 report had the floor-vote target slipping toward August 6.
  • NEW — the merged text is reportedly imminent. CoinDesk (July 9, citing people briefed on negotiations): a unified draft combining the Senate Banking and Agriculture texts — "more than 70 pages of text added," leaning harder into consumer protections — may drop as soon as the week of July 13, with advocates expecting floor action as soon as the week of July 20. Corroborated by The Crypto Times (July 10) and consistent with American Banker's independent July 9 reporting — but the scoop itself is a single primary reporting thread (other coverage largely derivative). This partially resolves the reconciliation question and puts a concrete date on TD Cowen's "week of July 13 or 20" path.
  • NEW NEGATIVE — the White House has NOT signed off on the merged text and has not engaged in the most recent negotiations (CoinDesk, July 9 — single source). The administration that demanded speed is currently outside the deal.
  • Ethics talks are still broken — now confirmed by two independent outlets (July 9). Collapsed June 9, not restarted. American Banker: the state-AGs-sue-DOJ compromise "dropped out of negotiations, and they haven't picked back up since." CoinDesk: "progress has slowed to a crawl." White House Crypto Council ED Patrick Witt's three-party talks have produced no principal-level restart; the White House still refuses any language singling out Trump. New wrinkle (single source — Crypto In America, July 9): Lummis floated letting state AGs sue exchanges for listing tokens tied to government officials. In committee history: Van Hollen's ethics amendment failed 11–13; Republicans withdrew state-AG enforcement and offered US-AG oversight (Democrats rejected it); Lummis's June 29 revised state-AG proposal appears to be the compromise that has since dropped out.
  • The June 30 ethics bomb, still live: OGE released Trump's 927-page disclosure on June 30: ~$1.4B of 2025 crypto income — $635M in TRUMP-memecoin royalties, $500M+ WLFI-related, $197M in USD1 stake sales (outlets differ on the WLFI token-sale split). Gillibrand: "there is no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" (exact wording via Yahoo's reporting; the substance — ethics language as a floor-vote prerequisite — is corroborated by crypto.news).
  • Section 604 / law enforcement — no movement since July 4, one new friendly voice. NOBLE's July 1 endorsement letter (the first from any law-enforcement organization) and the Major County Sheriffs' July 4 move to neutral still stand; the National Sheriffs' Association, FOP, NAPO, the district attorneys (NDAA), and IACP remain opposed or silent — no new endorsements. New: Sen. Ron Wyden (D-OR) publicly backed Section 604's developer protections on July 8 (Crypto In America, July 9; echoed by CoinDesk — single-source on the date and details) — a Democratic voice defending the provision police oppose, but not a committed cloture vote.
  • The vote math: still only Gallego and Alsobrooks on record — and both explicitly condition floor votes on enforceable ethics language. Warner and Cortez Masto still condition support on law-enforcement sign-off — not given. With Hawley and Paul expected R defections, that's ~51 reliable Republican votes → 7–9 Democrats needed. Firmly committed: zero. Analyst reads (American Banker, July 9 — single outlet quoting named analysts): Compass Point's Groshans sees "north of 65 votes" if ethics is resolved; CFRA's Schardin says the bill needs significant progress "in the next week or two."
  • The stablecoin-yield fight is now closed off rather than resolved (single source — American Banker, July 9): Banking Chairman Tim Scott rejected bank lobbyists' request to change yield-related restrictions on the floor; no further negotiations planned. Coinbase's ~$1.35B/yr USDC-rewards structure survives in the text as-is — the ABA loses this round.
  • Trump himself is still a signature risk: his threat to withhold his signature on a housing bill unless Congress passes the SAVE Act persists — a hostage play that squeezes scarce floor time and puts non-trivial signature risk on CLARITY itself.
  • Newly flagged as ALSO unresolved (CoinDesk, July 9): federal preemption and the SEC/CFTC commissioner-seat questions — a mild negative surprise on top of ethics.
  • Still true from June, still load-bearing: the House passed H.R. 3633 294-134 on July 17, 2025 (bipartisan); Senate Banking advanced its separate substitute 15-9 on May 14, 2026; the recess staff reconciliation appears to have produced the merged draft now reported imminent. House fast-follow plan (companion bill, no conference — Rep. Dusty Johnson) unchanged. A House FSC hearing on digital-asset market-structure gaps is confirmed for July 17 (committee event page); one outlet says it will be held in New York (single-source detail, unverified).

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • The market has stopped believing "delayed, not dead": Kalshi's new 2027–28 ladder prices passage by April 2027 at 41¢ and by January 2028 at just 56¢. If the smart money thought this was a pure timing problem, those long-dated contracts would be far richer.
  • The calendar is confirmed brutal: ~19 usable days, cloture already filed on the NDAA (not CLARITY), judicial nominations stacked first, and still no floor date. The week-of-July-20 "target" is advocates' expectation, not a schedule.
  • The dealmaker isn't at the table: the White House hasn't signed off on the merged text and skipped the latest negotiations. A 70-page rewrite tilting toward consumer protections could as easily lose Republican votes as gain Democratic ones — and Trump is already playing signature-hostage on another bill.
  • The ethics fight got harder, not easier: the only compromise on the table dropped out of negotiations entirely, the $1.4B presidential crypto disclosure is a gift to every Democrat who wants to say no, and Gillibrand's "no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" meets a White House that refuses any Trump-specific language — immovable object, irresistible force, 19 days.
  • The midterms are still the real wall: miss August 7 and September belongs to election politics. Worst case — Congress resets in January and the Senate starts its bill over.
