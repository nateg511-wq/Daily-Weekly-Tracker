The Claim

The thesis as of July 8, 2026, and where I differ from the consensus

The crowd: whipsawing. Polymarket's 2026-signing odds hit a YTD low of 39% on July 1 (the trading day after Trump's $1.4B crypto-income disclosure dropped June 30), spiked to ~56% over the July 4 weekend on law-enforcement news, and settled at 46–47% by July 7–8. The loud story: "no cloture motion, ~19 days, it's dead."

Me: draft ~52% for the next month — a coin flip, honestly — but still ~92% that a crypto rules law exists within 3 years.

My edge: unchanged since June — the crowd keeps pricing a timing problem like a death problem. The House did its half a year ago (294-134). The Section 604 police wall just cracked: the first law-enforcement endorsement ever landed July 3, and the big sheriffs went neutral July 4. The whole fight has narrowed to one variable — an ethics deal — and one-variable problems get solved or slipped, not killed.

The Facts

As of July 8, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page
  • The calendar, exactly: no cloture motion filed and no floor date as of July 8 — the bill has sat at Senate Calendar No. 423 since June 1. The Senate returns from the July 4 recess Monday, July 13; the last session day before the August break is Friday, August 7 — roughly 19–20 working days, and the NDAA eats week one. TD Cowen (June 29): procedural steps the week of July 13 or July 20, with July 24 the critical date so the House can act before its own recess. One July 7 report says the floor-vote target has slipped toward August 6.
  • The June 30 ethics bomb. OGE released Trump's 927-page disclosure on June 30: ~$1.4B of 2025 crypto income — $635M in TRUMP-memecoin royalties, $500M+ WLFI-related, $197M in USD1 stake sales (outlets differ on the WLFI token-sale split). Warren and other Democrats escalated. Gillibrand: "there is no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" (exact wording via Yahoo's reporting; the substance — ethics language as a floor-vote prerequisite — is corroborated by crypto.news).
  • Ethics talks are still broken. Collapsed June 9, not publicly restarted as of July 8 — "the last issue to be finalized." White House Crypto Council ED Patrick Witt is running three-party talks, but the White House refuses any language singling out Trump. In committee: Van Hollen's ethics amendment failed 11–13; Republicans withdrew state-AG enforcement and offered US-AG oversight (Democrats rejected it); Lummis floated a revised state-AG proposal June 29.
  • The Section 604 wall is cracking. After White House meetings ~June 29–July 1: NOBLE's endorsement letter to Thune and Schumer — the first from any law-enforcement organization — was dated July 1 (reported July 2–3); on July 4, the Major County Sheriffs of America moved from opposed to neutral. Still opposed: the National Sheriffs' Association, FOP, the district attorneys (NDAA), and IACP.
  • The vote math: still only Gallego and Alsobrooks on record — and both now say their committee votes don't guarantee floor votes without an ethics deal. Warner and Cortez Masto still condition support on law-enforcement sign-off. With Hawley and Paul expected R defections, that's ~51 reliable Republican votes → 7–9 Democrats needed. Firmly committed: zero.
  • The stablecoin-yield fight is unresolved: the ABA says the bill's pass-through-yield language loopholes GENIUS's issuer-interest ban; Coinbase's ~$1.35B/yr in USDC rewards is what's at stake.
  • New: Trump himself is a signature risk. He's threatening to withhold his signature on a housing bill unless Congress passes the SAVE Act — a hostage play that squeezes scarce floor time and puts non-trivial signature risk on CLARITY itself.
  • Still true from June, still load-bearing: the House passed H.R. 3633 294-134 on July 17, 2025 (bipartisan); Senate Banking advanced its separate substitute 15-9 on May 14, 2026; Banking–Agriculture staff are reconciling committee texts over the recess.
  • Odds board (sentiment only): Polymarket 46–47% (Jul 7–8, after the 39% → ~56% whipsaw) · Kalshi as of July 7 (via Deadspin): passage by end-of-July ~6¢, by end-of-2026 ~46¢ (a July 1 snapshot had July at 0.1% and August at 13% — prices whipsawed with the news) · Galaxy cut 60% → 50% (June 29: "Thune must announce floor time by early July" or it slips to September/midterms) · JPMorgan <50% (in-house) · Jefferies (June 30) flagged Polymarket at 48%, down from 70% mid-May — a market price it cited, not a house estimate · TD Cowen: "far from assured" before the midterms.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • The calendar is worse than June: ~19 usable days, the NDAA eats the first week, no cloture motion filed, and the leader is still only "looking at" floor time (June 29). Galaxy's own early-July deadline for a Thune announcement has already passed unmet.
  • The ethics fight got harder, not easier: a $1.4B presidential crypto disclosure is a gift to every Democrat who wants to say no. Gillibrand's "no CLARITY Act without an ethics provision" meets a White House that refuses any Trump-specific language — immovable object, irresistible force, 19 days.
  • The signer is now a risk: Trump is already playing signature-hostage on another bill. A Senate that grinds out 60 votes could still watch the bill die on his desk.
  • The midterms are still the real wall: miss August 7 and September belongs to election politics. Worst case — Congress resets in January and the Senate starts its bill over.
