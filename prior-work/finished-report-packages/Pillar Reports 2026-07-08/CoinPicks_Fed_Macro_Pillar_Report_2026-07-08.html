The Claim

The thesis, and where I differ from the consensus — updated July 8, 2026

The crowd: "Inflation and the Fed are back in play" (Yardeni, July 8). Futures price >75% odds of at least one hike before year-end (up from 58% at the start of June), a ~58% September hike after the Iran oil spike, and a ~73% hold for July 29. Cuts are off the board. The loud story: hawkish Warsh + Hormuz oil = crypto stays dead indefinitely.

Me: near-term the crowd is right — my draft P is 35–36 out to 3 months, if anything below consensus gloom. Liquidity is the tide and it's still out. My edge is the sequencing: the July 2 jobs report shows the labor market visibly cracking (+57K with −74K of revisions and participation at a 2021 low), Warsh has publicly staked his chairmanship on price stability, so the Fed will hike into that weakness before it eases — the worst near-term order of events for crypto, but a policy-error hike raises the amplitude of the easing cycle that follows.

My edge: the crowd is pricing the hike; almost nobody is pricing what comes after it. QT is dead, bank reserves are down ~$418B in a year, the RRP is empty — the plumbing is quietly tightening toward the point where the Fed has to add liquidity again. That's why the 3-year P holds at 61 while everything nearer gets marked down.

The Facts

As of July 8, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page. ★ = new since the June 19 check
  • The labor market cracked. June payrolls +57K vs ~115K consensus (released July 2); April revised −31K and May −43K — −74K combined. Leisure/hospitality −61K. Unemployment "fell" to 4.2% only because participation dropped 0.3pp to 61.5% — the lowest since March 2021. Wages +0.3% m/m.
  • Core inflation is going the wrong way. May core PCE 3.4% y/y (+0.3% m/m, released June 25–26) — the highest since October 2023, up from 3.3% at the June check. Headline PCE 4.1%. June CPI lands July 14; the Cleveland Fed nowcast says ~3.96% headline — cooling from 4.2%, still hot.
  • The market now prices the hike, not the cut. As of July 6–7, futures put >75% on at least one hike before year-end 2026 (vs ~60–66% on June 19). After the oil spike, September hike odds hit ~58%. July 29 is a ~73% hold. The July 2 jobs miss briefly swung it dovish (July hike odds 29%→22%) — oil swung it right back.
  • Iran re-armed the inflation scare. July 7: Iran hit commercial vessels in Hormuz and the US revoked the Iran oil-sale waiver (wind-down by July 17); US retaliatory strikes followed in the early hours of July 8. Brent settled $74.16 (+3%) July 7 and traded ~$76–78 July 8. The spike came too late for the June CPI data — it lands in July's.
  • Warsh is uniformly hawkish. July 1 at Sintra (first international appearance): inflation "too elevated," no hint on July, and he joined Lagarde/Bailey/Macklem against forward guidance. And the line he's staked the chairmanship on — "This Committee will deliver price stability" — comes from the June 17 FOMC statement and press conference (recirculated in July 6 coverage). Rate policy is his primary tool; balance-sheet reduction ambitions are intact but gradual — no asset sales imminent.
  • Rates and the dollar confirm the squeeze. 10Y 4.53% July 8 (one-month high, vs 4.46% June 19); 2Y ~4.21%. DXY ~101.0–101.1 — holding near, ~0.7pt below, its late-June 13-month peak of 101.8 (CNBC July 8 called it a one-week high). The $58B 3Y auction (July 7) was strong — reported as stopping through with the highest indirect take since April (per TFTC; auction internals not independently confirmed) — dollar-asset demand is intact.
  • Policy rate and balance sheet: still 3.50–3.75%, still ~$6.74T flat. QT remains done (H.4.1, week of June 24–25: ~$6.736T). But the plumbing is quietly tightening: TGA ~$919B (elevated = drain), RRP essentially empty ($2B), bank reserves $2.951T — down from $3.369T a year ago. Then: +$223.5M July 2 (ending a second 10-day outflow streak), +$265.7M July 6 (largest daily inflow in over a month; IBIT +$209.4M), and a third straight green day July 7 (+$21–47M depending on tracker). AUM ~$77B, up from a June 30 trough that providers put at ~$71–73B (methodologies differ).
  • Prices under the headwind: BTC opened $63,318 July 8 and slid toward ~$62K on the strikes (Bitstamp daily close $62,274; BTC had already printed a ~$61.7K 21-month low on July 6, pre-escalation); ETH ~$1,742–1,769. Roughly where it sat on June 19 (~$62.5K) — stabilization, not recovery.

The Prize

Illustrative only — not price targets, not advice

The canonical anchor: 2020-21 ZIRP + QE ran BTC ~$3,800 → $69,044 (~15–18×) — a unique COVID/zero-rate/open-ended-QE setup, not a repeatable base case. The 2024-25 cuts ran ~$60K → a ~$126K ATH (Oct 6, 2025) — but that top came on a non-Fed tariff shock and has since halved to ~$62K. From here, a retest of the $126K high ≈ +100%.

Discount it hard, twice. First: easing is not an automatic catalyst — the last cycle topped on a non-Fed shock, and a Dec 10, 2025 cut drew a sell-the-fact dip. Second: the pivot that unlocks any of this just got pushed further out — the Fed is priced to hike before it eases. The Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the 3-year lean is worth acting on when the tide finally turns.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case my bearish lean is wrong
  • The labor market may force the Fed's hand faster than anyone prices. +57K with −74K of revisions and participation at a 2021 low is not "solid but cooling" anymore — one more print like that and the hike is off the table. The market went dovish within hours of the July 2 report; only oil dragged it back.
  • The inflation data could break my way this month. June CPI nowcasts ~3.96% — cooling — and the oil spike missed the June window. A soft July 14 CPI plus a soft July 25 core PCE flips the board before the July 28–29 FOMC.
  • Real money is stepping back in at $62K. Three green ETF days in four since July 2, including the biggest daily inflow in over a month. Capitulation-scale outflow months often mark exhaustion rather than the middle of the move.
  • Crypto keeps decoupling from the Fed — symmetrically. The Oct 2025 top and the May 2026 selloff weren't Fed-driven; crypto can rally without a pivot just as it fell without one. (Though note: BTC and ETH fell on the July 8 strikes — this week it's trading macro.)
  • Latent liquidity positives are queued: Waller publicly fighting scarce-reserve regimes, a $919B TGA that eventually gets spent down, and reserves drifting toward levels that historically force balance-sheet support. (The eSLR recalibration — once on this list — was finalized Nov 2025 and took effect April 1, 2026; that card has been played.) The plumbing that's tightening today can loosen without a single rate cut.
