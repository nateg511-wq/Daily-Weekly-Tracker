The Claim

The thesis, and where I differ from the consensus — updated July 8, 2026

The crowd: "the ceasefire is over" — Trump said it himself on July 8. Missiles into tankers, the Strait of Hormuz declared closed, US strikes on 80+ targets inside Iran, IRGC drones at US bases in Bahrain and Kuwait. The loud story is pure risk-off: stay out until it's over.

Me: near-term I'm with the bears for once — 40% good-for-crypto at 1 month, because an active shoot-back loop with re-sanctioned oil is no place to be a hero. But the arc holds: 83% over three years. The dip is still a buy — it just isn't fully formed yet.

My edge: the market itself is telling you how desensitized it is — the July 8 escalation bought a ~2–3% BTC dip to ~$61.7k, not a crash. The risk isn't the headlines the crowd fears; it's the quiet paths — a US-casualty event, a formal MoU death by ~Aug 16, a confirmed full Hormuz closure. I watch those, not the noise.

The Facts

As of July 8, 2026 — hard numbers only, sources on the last page. ★ = new since June 19
  • Tanker attacks, night of July 6–7: the IRGC fired ≥2 missiles at commercial ships in/near the Strait of Hormuz. Two merchant vessels hit that night: Qatari LNG carrier Al Rekayyat (struck ~8nm off Limah, Oman — engine-room fire, crew abandoned ship) and Saudi crude tanker Wedyan (damaged); a third vessel was hit within 24 hours — three hit in total — and four more tankers turned back rather than transit. Tehran did not officially claim responsibility.
  • This was the second round in ten days. On June 27 an Iranian one-way attack drone hit the tanker M/T Kiku (Panama-flagged, 2M+ bbl of crude); the US struck targets in Iran the same day (CENTCOM release), and the IRGC hit US sites in Kuwait and Bahrain on June 28. The ceasefire has now cracked at least three times since April (June 7–8, June 27–28, July 6–8).
  • US response, late July 7 → July 8: CENTCOM struck 80+ Iranian targets early July 8 (hours after the ship attacks) — air defenses, coastal radar, C2, anti-ship missile sites, 60+ IRGC small boats; explosions on Qeshm Island and at Sirik. CENTCOM called it roughly 8× larger than the June 27 round. Later July 8 the IRGC claimed attacks on 85 US military sites in Bahrain and Kuwait — its second strike on Gulf bases in ~10 days (missiles/drones; Bahrain sirens 3x; no casualties reported) — and vowed to "retaliate in a harsher way."
  • Ceasefire declared over: at the NATO summit in Ankara on July 8, Trump: "I think it's over… I'll let our wonderful negotiators keep talking if they want, but I don't see it." Additional US strikes were underway Wednesday night.
  • Hormuz squeeze: Iran declared the strait shut back on ~June 22; traffic had been recovering (a widened route opened June 27) until the July 6–8 attacks left it effectively closed to commercial shipping as of July 8 (US says some vessels still transiting). Unofficial tracker data (straits.live): throughput ~39–41% of pre-crisis as of July 5 — pre-attack — with VLCC war-risk premiums ~8× normal. Insurance Journal (July 8): war-risk rates ~3% of hull value, up from 2% a week earlier ("probably at 5% at the least" per one underwriter), with underwriters advising owners to pause Hormuz voyages; trade press puts Hormuz-transit VLCC fixtures at ~$440–470K/day.
  • Oil re-sanctioned: the US Treasury revoked the MoU waiver allowing Iranian oil sales on July 7 — wind-down completes by July 17.
  • Oil price: Brent settled +3% at $74.16 July 7, then +5.2% at $78.02 July 8 (WTI +4.4%, $73.52) — ~+9% in two sessions, yet still below the ~$80 June 19 baseline (oil had faded to ~$72 during the de-escalation).
  • Diplomacy — resumed, then stalled: after the June 19 Switzerland cancellation, talks restarted (a "Lake Lucerne Summit," then indirect technical talks in Doha, June 30–July 2; Vance July 1: "going well"; concluded July 2 with "positive progress" claimed but no breakthrough). Doha did produce two concrete outcomes: agreement to release the first $3B tranche of ~$6B in Qatar-held frozen Iranian funds, and an MoU-violations reporting channel. Sticking points: Hormuz transit fees (Iran July 4: will "definitely" charge; US rejects) and the rest of the frozen funds. The sides agreed in principle to reconvene after the Khamenei funeral processions (July 4–9) — the July 8 US strikes reportedly broke a promised funeral-period pause — but as of July 8 no date is set; Iran's FM says talks "will not commence if threats continue"; Tehran has not formally declared the MoU dead. The 60-day window runs to ~Aug 16.
  • Israel is out of this round: no direct Israel–Iran exchange since June 7–8, and Israel was not a participant in the July 7–8 strikes (Netanyahu visited Haifa naval base July 7, "eye to eye" with Trump). Israel–Hezbollah simmers on — Rubio announced an Israel–Lebanon "framework deal" June 26 — but it is not the July driver.
  • Crypto now: BTC slipped from $62,657 in Asia hours July 8 toward ~$62K (−1.7 to −2.5% by outlet; some trackers print high-$61Ks), selloff explicitly attributed to the "ceasefire over" remarks and the strikes. Context that matters: BTC had already printed ~$61.7K on July 6 — a 21-month low — before this escalation, so the war headlines pushed price only back toward a level it had just visited. ~$450M total liquidations (~$350M in altcoins); SOL wiped out its July rally.
  • The odds: Polymarket "US invades Iran before 2027" jumped 11.5% → 14.5% on the strikes (independently reported); "Iran withdraws from MoU talks by Aug 15" at 26% and "US–Iran final nuclear deal by Dec 31" at 35.5% (read directly off Polymarket July 8 — live snapshots, they move).
  • The baseline (still true, still load-bearing): June 2025 "12-Day War" — BTC dipped to a ~$98,225 intraday low (June 22, 2025), reclaimed $107k+ by June 25, a fast V. The far larger 2026 regime-change war (Feb 28 → Apr 8, 2026; Khamenei killed Feb 28, confirmed by Iran March 1) still didn't crater crypto on Iran-specific grounds.

The Prize

Illustrative only — not price targets, not advice

The upside is still the "buy-the-invasion" rebound. Anchors: Russia–Ukraine (Feb 24, 2022) — BTC fell ~8% to a ~$34,300 intraday low, posted a +14.5% up-day on Feb 28, ended ~+25–29% above the low within a week (~+38% by late March). June 2025 rhymed: ~$98k dip, fully recovered within a week.

The test is live right now. July 8's dip was only ~2–3% to ~$61.7k — a desensitized market means shallower dips and smaller Vs. The bigger prize is the settlement scenario: a signed deal plus the reported ~$300B reconstruction fund re-pricing the whole region after oil re-sanctioning. Still a thin sample; the Prize doesn't feed the math — it tells you what the lean is worth acting on.

The Other Side

The strongest honest case this is wrong
  • BTC is a risk asset here, not a safe haven — and the oil-shock path now has numbers. Goldman's April scenario: Brent averages >$100 in 2026 if Hormuz stays largely shut another month ($120 Q3 / $115 Q4); JPMorgan: $120–130 if disruption persists through Q3, ~$150 in a full-closure worst case. That is an inflation shock — crypto falls with equities, the opposite of the dip-buy.
  • It keeps breaking. The ceasefire has now cracked at least three times since April — June 7–8, June 27–28, and July 6–8's "it's over." The rounds are getting closer together and bigger. Each one, "time heals" gets directly contradicted.
  • The shallow dip may be complacency, not resilience. Markets are pricing a contained flare-up (BTC −2–3%, Brent $78 — below the June baseline). That pricing is fragile to a US-casualty event, a formal MoU withdrawal, or a confirmed full closure — any one repricing gaps down, not drifts.
  • Thin sample, still. The bullish long-term read rests on ~2 dip-and-recover events, both in a bull regime — geopolitical shocks don't always V-recover.
